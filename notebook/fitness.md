Goal: keep up with inter group on saturday group ride. 30km/h average on the bike.

Strategy: Increase training volume for cycling and running, to improve cycling-specific adaptions and cardiovascular adaptions while keeping variety in training.

Lead Measures: 3x runs per week, 4x cycles per week.

- Tuesday commute

- Thursday run
- Friday commute

## Nutrition

High protein high carb low fat diet where possible. The modern australian diet tends to high in fat and low in protein, mostly due to convenience: crisps, corn chips, fast food (and asian food especially). Culturally we pride ourselves on having decadent and rich foods because they go viral on social media, but an actual nutrition strategy needs to stick to the boring basics: lean protein, clean carbs, high fibre, low fat.

Some low-lift changes:

- avoid pastries :(
- switch to skim milk (coffee and cereal) or opt for espresso/americano
- avoid deep-fried foods
- switch to high fibre cereal (wheat bran)
- higher grade beef mince is 5% fat vs low grade which is 11-15% fat
- lean cuts of meat (chicken breast)
- grilling roasting steaming instead of frying
- trim visible fat before cooking
- avoid cream-based and oil-based sauces

### Philosophy

- Every workout needs to be high quality. Don't grind out a mediocre workout. Example: a planned tempo run which ends up feeling sluggish, achey, stiff etc. after 10 minutes means you should go home. I treat that as an overtrain signal. Have a full meal, get proper sleep, and try again tomorrow, refreshed.
- Running take a significant amount of injury management. You need to be always paying attention to your body. The stresses on your joins from running can easily progress into full blown injury if you're not careful.
- Volume (at quality) is key. To achieve volume you need nutrition and recovery.

## Risk Management/Mitigation

### Weather

You can run in the rain apparently. But it seems to ruin your shoes. idk if i should have a separate wet weather pair or what.

Skipping rainy days can mean missing a lot of training sessions, especially in Sydney.

apparently cotton is bad for rain because of how it hold moisture close to your skin. makes sense. but cotton socks are so much more comfortable than synthetics. tbh i get worse blisters from my synthetic lululemon socks.

can you get sick from running in the rain? well it's actually the cold which increases your risk of getting sick. and when you run, you're pretty warm. so what matters is not spending too much time "cold", i.e. don't walk home for 10 minutes after the run. Just run all the way home and get inside asap. hot shower straight away or at the very least dry off.

what about shoes? best bet is to take them off as soon as you can and stuff them with towels to let them air dry. i have those cheapy microfibres you can get in a bulk pack.

would be great to have a running shell jacket with venting but seems like they're not that common in store? weird. can find them at new balance and rebel or decathlon etc.
