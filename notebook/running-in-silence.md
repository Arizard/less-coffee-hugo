My life is full of multi tasking. Distractions. It's overstimulating. But running is meditation. Most people I know run with music, as if to make the discomfort pass quickly. I'm the opposite, I want it to feel like it's dragging on. Time away from screens and from home.

Running is the one time I get to unplug everything (except for my Apple Watch). It’s a break from screen time and distraction. It’s switching off. I could listen to music but the point is to enjoy with the silence. The repetition. Ironically, physical work ends up being mental rest.

I listened to music on the run for a long time. I had tried running on-and-off for about 3 years, with music. I was really into _Neck Deep_ for a while because they had a music kit in _Counter-Strike_. Then I had a playlist called [_2000s Gamer_](https://music.apple.com/au/playlist/2000s-gamer/pl.u-KVXBq0VCz59lr4) (also listened to a lot while playing Counter-Strike). My running progress was non-existent, and I was injuring myself frequently. Then I forgot to charge my wireless headphones (where did our obsession with wireless come from? Anyway...) and was forced to run in silence. That was when I finally had my breakthrough. It was like stepping through the front door of your house after a long, loud night out. My attention was focused only on running with quality.

Most people are better off not listening to music in public, especially in public parks. If you're away from the road, it's actually pleasant to listen to the sounds around you. Immerse yourself in the mundane, I promise it's not as boring as you think.

People on the footpath frustrate me often. Is it their first day out in public? They don't know which side of the footpath to walk on, walk three or four abreast, zig-zag like they're trying to evade something, or they pretend not to notice the way their dog's leash cordons the way for everyone else. One time, towards the end 40-ish minute run, someone stepped blindly backwards into the last sliver of free space their group had taken up. Obviously I am a lunatic, and it was too late to change direction, so in that moment I charged through their bag. I heard them exclaim in surprise. Mission accomplished. I continued on my run without looking back.

This is just me problems. Plenty of people actually run better with music. IDK man.

Most people are better off not listening to music in public, especially in public parks. I'd even go as far as saying that beginner runners should run without music until you get the technique down. Besides, if you're away from the road, it's actually pleasant to listen to the sounds around you. Immerse yourself in the mundane, I promise it's not as boring as you think.

Running is when I unplug myself. It's meditation. A break from screen time and distractions. I _want_ the time to drag on, so I can savour it. In that moment, life is someone else's responsibility, and my job becomes simple: keep going. What began for me as physical work ended up being mental rest.
