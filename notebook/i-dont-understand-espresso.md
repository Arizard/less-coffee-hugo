Some light roast coffees (perhaps all?) taste a lot better with a light extraction, e.g. 40-50ml in 19s. Basically a turbo shot. But dark roasts taste good with a slower extraction, maybe 25s. So why? Could it be that the bright fruity notes come through at short extractions, while the dark bitter chocolate notes come through at long extractions?

How i make a latte

- heat up coffee machine, rinse the basket for 10s
- measure out 20g of whole beans using a scale
- turn grinder to finest setting, grind for 1-2 sec with empty hopper to clear the retained grounds, reset to 13 (the setting i've configured for espresso)
- grind 20g of beans into the basket
- WDT tool to break up any clumps
- spinning leveller to level the surface of the grounds
- tamp with body weight to compress grounds
- add puck screen (idk if this does anything tbh)
- tighten
- place cup, pull espresso. I use the same cup every time, so i can eyeball the volume rather than rely on timing the shot. depends on roast, it can be anywhere from 15s to 25s.
- remove cup, switch to steam mode (single boiler). flush steam wand
- go pour some milk into a pitcher
- flush steam wand again. wait 10s
- one final flush
- now start steaming the milk. ignore the advice to create a vortex, it's a 2-hole head so i put the tip into the centre of the milk, so it creates two opposing vortexes.
- build bubbles for 10-15s and then dunk the head a little lower to start incorporating the air.
- hand-test side of the pitcher for temperature. once it's too hot to touch i stop.
- swish the milk around (idk if this helps)
- pour into cup, maybe attempt latte art, usually fail because i didn't steam it right :)
- done! now i have a latte
- pour excess milk into sink, rinse pitcher
- remove portafilter, grounds go into knock box, rinse puck screen and basket
- reattach portafilter, turn off steam mode, flush until no more steam
- put puck screen on paper towel to dry
- enjoy

notice how i'm kinda ignoring shot timing. i just eyeball the volume. IMO the volume has more influence on taste than timing does, and is easier to get right in the moment. timing is just a heuristic while online guides seem to think it's more important than it really is.

always smell the shot after you extract. build the feedback loop. it's way easier to smell the notes than to taste them
