If you want to be a high agency person, you need to bet on yourself. Don't try
to rationalise everything. You'll never have all the answers. Unexpected things
will happen and you'll just have to figure it out as you go.

I describe it as "betting" for a reason: you're taking a risk.

Bigger bets have
bigger potential payoffs, but also risk bigger losses.

It's not the same as pretending bad stuff doesn't happen, by the way.

It's not the same as deluding yourself into thinking bad stuff doesn't happen.
Bad stuff always happens, but a high agency person knows that and mentally
commits to dealing with it. Best case, the bad stuff doesn't happen at all.
Worst case, it happens and you deal with it. High agency people take calculated
risks, and sometimes that calculation is just a gut feeling that whatever bad
stuff might happen can be dealt with later.


- Why is self direction so hard? Because you have to make the decisions
  yourself. Why are decisions hard to make? Because we’re conditioned to feel
  like we need to know all the info before making a decision. Why do we feel
  like that? Honestly probably school or certain workplaces. But is it really
  that bad to decide before knowing all the info? Can you always know all the
  info? No of course not, so it’s better to think of it as risk vs reward.
  Calculated uncertainty. There’s a cost, an upside and a downside.

When I was a fitness instructor I bet on myself all the time. Betting on myself
to teach a good class, hit the cue timings right, keep my balance, not pass out,
etc. Many would never do that job because of fear—public speaking, self esteem,
etc. But to do that job I had to silence the inner voice of doubt. It was
delusion, in a way, but it got me through. I had to bet on myself because if
something unpredictable happens in a class, I'd just have to deal with it on the
spot, that was the only appropriate solution.
